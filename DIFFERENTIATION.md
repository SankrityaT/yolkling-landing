# "how is this different?" the honest answers (for when reddit grills)

reddit will instantly ask "isn't this just X". have these ready. all honest, all in your voice, no em dashes. rewrite to the specific comment, never paste cold.

## THE one that matters: it's actually social, none of them are
every other virtual pet is you, alone, with a pet in a box. yolkling is the only one where your creature wanders off ON ITS OWN and visits your FRIENDS' creatures, gets into unscripted nonsense, and comes back with stories. your guy went to your friend's place, they hit it off, now they "hang out". your creature and your sister's creature are somehow inseparable. it's a tiny living world made of the people you actually know, and the funny emergent moments are the product. finch, tamagotchi, pou, forest, all of them are single player. lead with this. it's the thing nobody else has and the thing people screenshot.

## vs Finch
finch is a solo self-care app where a bird reacts to you doing tasks and journaling. yolkling grows from your actual life (sleep, water, sunlight, texting people back), not from chores inside an app, and the big one: it's social. your creature visits your friends' creatures. finch's bird never leaves finch. yolkling's whole magic is that yours does.

## vs Tamagotchi / Pou
closed boxes. you feed a pet that lives entirely inside the app, alone, disconnected from everything (and pou is ad-heavy and basically abandoned). yolkling is tied to your real wellbeing AND it's a shared social world, your creature and your friends' creatures actually interact. it's the tamagotchi that grew up, made friends, and has no ads.

## vs Forest
forest is a solo timer that kills a tree to punish you. yolkling never punishes or guilt trips, it just lights up when you do good things. and again, forest is one person staring at a timer. yolkling is your creature out visiting the people you love.

## vs Opal (and Freedom, one-sec, the blockers)
opal is the most serious app in our lane so this one matters. opal is a BLOCKER, it locks you out of apps with friction (its deep focus mode literally cannot be turned off, deleting the app does not even unblock it), and people pay $100 a year for it. that actually proves two great things for us: the screen time entitlement is gettable, and people really do pay to get off their phone. but opal is the stick and yolkling is the carrot. we block nothing. you do not lock yourself out, you just have a little creature that thrives when you live well and that you do not want to let down. blockers have a churn problem, people disable them, resent the nagging, bounce off the price. you do not rage quit a pet you love the way you rage quit a lockbox. opal is also solo and utilitarian, yolkling is social and cute. opal is for the hardcore discipline crowd. yolkling is for everyone else who wants off their phone but does not want to be policed. someone could honestly run both.

## vs habit trackers (Habitica etc.)
those are spreadsheets with xp bolted on, solo, you doing data entry. yolkling leads with a character you care about and a little social world it lives in. the habits are invisible plumbing. you're not managing a checklist, you're watching your little guy go have a life.

## "how do you make money / what's the catch"
free to hatch forever, no ads, never selling data. only money is cheap optional cosmetics (hats, rooms, rare colours) and you can earn most for free by living well. one person just covering server costs, not trying to get rich. that's the whole model.

## "isn't this just another dopamine app pretending to be wellness"
opposite. it's built to want less of your attention, it gets sleepy when you doomscroll. and the social part isn't a feed you scroll, it's your creature going and doing things on its own and leaving you a postcard. you check in, you smile, you put the phone down.

## "is this AI slop / another AI app"
no ai gimmicks. creatures are simple shapes generated procedurally in code, that's how one person makes millions of unique ones without an art team. it's a genuinely cute thing in a little social world, not an ai feature checklist.

## "the social thing needs everyone to be on it"
it's lovely solo too (you raise your own), but the social layer is exactly what makes it spread, your creature visiting a friend's is a built in reason to tell that friend about it. the network IS the marketing. that's a feature, not a problem.

## the one-liner if you only get one sentence
it's the only virtual pet that grows from your real life and then wanders off to visit your friends' pets and gets into stuff, the tamagotchi that has a social life.
